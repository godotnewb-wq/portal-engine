# engine/scene

One player turn, in at most three LLM calls (`runTurn`):

1. **Director** (`director.ts`): reads everything about the scene and returns a JSON plan (`plan.ts`; its checked shape is in `planShape.ts`). The game checks it against the scene and the ruleset (`validatePlan`); an unreadable reply means the game plans the turn itself (`fallbackPlan`), at no extra call. The plan's `moves` are the turn's conversation in order (see **The plan**, below); `exchange` is derived from them, one line per move, for read-outs (`planLines`, `lineText`) and the beat trace, where a dialogue beat links to its `line:<n>` and to the check its move `needs`. Reactions are silent: quoted speech of three or more words in a narration beat is a `speech-in-narration` problem, and if the rewrite still has it, salvage cuts the words and keeps the action (`withoutSpeech`). A speaker's beats in a row, with only narration between, are one planned line written in parts. Anyone silent for `QUIET_TURNS` turns is listed in the director's turn prompt with their trade and aims, so the director can see whether the conversation touches them; whether they speak stays its call (no quotas).
2. **Game resolution** (`resolve.ts`): rolls the plan's checks with the seeded dice.
3. **Writer** (`writer.ts`): sees the plan as a brief, this turn's speakers' portraits and voice cards, its style principles and the writer's stage (the pack's tone and the names it may use); streams one beat per line with hidden tags (`beats.ts`). A beat is speech or narration, never both: a dialogue beat holds only the quoted words, and a character's actions are narration beats that name them. A line that still mixes the two is split by code into beats in order (`ParsedReply.reshaped`, `TurnTrace.writer.reshaped`), with no rewrite.
4. **Code checks** (`checks.ts`): speaking or acting for the player, unplanned or unknown speakers, over-used phrases (the engine's and the pack's), beat length and count. With a codex, `loreProblems` adds the name checks: a `wrong-name` (the fix names the right one) and an `invented-name` (use an existing name or a description); salvage lets names stand. The secret-leak check was removed on 2026-11-12: a secret can come out through anyone or anything the story allows, and conflicts with established lore are milestone 8's lore audit to sort out. The pack's names are indexed once (`packNames`). The director's lore lines get the same name check: a line with a wrong or invented name is dropped as a correction. Any hit means one rewrite; a rewrite that still fails is salvaged by code, never a fourth call.
5. **Apply** (`turn.ts`): the turn goes into the state with moods, comfort, expressions and departures. Departures and the scene's end come from the plan alone. `acceptTags` decides which of the writer's tags count: a `[LEAVES]` tag moves no one, and when salvage cut every beat a character appeared in, their `EXPRESSION`, `MOOD` and `COMFORT` tags are set aside. Each tag set aside goes into the trace's `tags.dropped` with why. The writer's brief lists who leaves this turn; a planned departure the kept beats don't show (no line by them, no narration naming them) gets one narration beat in the pack's words (`showDepartures`, the ruleset's `narration.departure`), traced as a `game` beat carrying `leaves:<id>`.

**Lore** (`lore.ts`, milestone 3): `turnLore` works out the turn's codex layers once (`@engine/codex`). The director opens with the **stage** (the pack's primer plus every common entry's stage line) in place of the bare primer, then gets the **scene-scoped layer** (the place's map chain, the factions and peoples of those present, linked entries as summaries) after the location, and the **triggered layer** (entries whose words came up in the input, the last turns or the summary) in its turn prompt. The writer gets the **writer's stage**: the tone and the names it may use. `turnLore` takes the player's lore budget overrides (`TurnOptions.loreBudgets`, from Settings or the bench config) over the pack's. The trace's `lore` lists the entry ids per layer, their rough tokens, and the budgets in effect with which the player set. The layers show each entry's id, so the director can pick **lore lines**: a turn-level pool of at most the turn's `loreLines` budget of plain facts (the ruleset's `codex.loreLines`, or the player's setting; with 0 the director isn't asked for any) (`{entry, who, line}`), apart from the moves. `validatePlan` checks them the way `canSay` checks facts: the entry was given this turn and isn't a secret; an insider entry's line needs a `who`, a speaker who knows it; failures are dropped and counted as corrections; a line over `LORE_LINE_WORDS` gives way to its entry's summary, also counted as a correction. Speakers may carry a `framing`, how they colour what they say about the world. The writer gets the lines under their own heading, to draw on if they fit (marked with who would say an insider one), and each speaker's framing on their line of the brief.

Every turn also returns a **turn trace** (`trace.ts`, milestone 2 A1): identity, the cast before and after, the director's full prompt and raw reply and how it was read, the plan after corrections, a breakdown of every check, the writer's and rewrite's prompts and replies with timings, the code findings and what salvage did, each beat with its source line and the plan item it carries out, the tags, and the cost. The engine builds it; the platform writes it (`@platform/logs`). A failed call throws a `TracedError` with the calls made so far.

**Assists and disclosure** (`resolve.ts`, milestone 4a; core rules §1, npc design §6): when a check resolves, `helpersFor` finds the party members present whose assist gate is met toward whoever rolls (the player, or another companion) and who have the ruleset's rank in the skill; `assistBonus` gives the bonus by how many help, never an edge, and the check line names them. `willingHelpers` puts the gate in words for the director ("Ada would back the player up"). A speaker who uses one of their own secrets in a move is checked by `wouldDisclose` against that secret's `disclose` minimums toward whoever the move is said to; a refusal drops the secret with a correction. The director's sheet says in words whether a secret with minimums would be told to the player now. A secret already said on screen, or already known to the player, isn't gated.

**The child guard** (`guard.ts`, director writer §5): when a character whose age stage the ruleset lists under `people.ages.minors` is present, `childGuard` adds an engine line to the director's and the writer's system prompts naming them and keeping them out of anything romantic or sexual; packs can't turn it off. The director's sheet header names a minor's age stage.

**Public API** (`@engine/scene`): `runTurn`, `TurnLlm`, `TurnEvent`, `TurnOutcome`, `TurnOptions` (turn id, log session, clock); `TurnTrace`, `BeatTrace`, `turnLore`, `TurnLore`, `loreTrace`, `LoreTrace`, `CheckTrace`, `CastSnapshot`, `planItems`; `promptSources` (the fixed texts the prompt version hashes); the prompt builders, `parsePlan`, `validatePlan`, `resolvePlan`, `parseReply`, `checkReply`, `loreProblems`, `packNames`, `applyTurn`, `WRITER_STYLE`, `WRITER_FORMAT`, `OVERUSED_PHRASES`, `BEATS`.

The templates are engine code; every world word in them comes from the pack (primer, tone, setting name, skills, difficulty words, meters, phrases).

**Depends on:** core, rules, characters, world, pack, state, memory.
**Design:** `director writer.md`; the redesign in `scene system redesign.md`. **Tuning:** `BEATS`, `MAX_SPEAKERS`, `MAX_CHECKS`, `RECENT_USE_TURNS`, `REPEAT_REWRITE` (code); the ruleset's `pressures`; the pack's tone, primer and phrases.

## The plan
The situation plan (`director.ts`, `plan.ts`, `facts.ts`, `writer.ts`; `director writer review.md`, option B; the only pipeline since milestone 3.6 removed the older line plan, which planned each line's point): the director plans what is true, wanted and at stake, and writes no prose or dialogue.
- `player_action` says what the player is doing and whether it is an uncertain attempt; a player check without one is dropped.
- Each speaker has a `goal` (what they want in this exchange) and a `pressure`: one of the ruleset's pressure words for how hard their aim for the scene presses. It moves at most one step a turn (`scene.pressures`). The writer sees the aim only above the weakest word.
- `keeps` lists the ids of the speaker's own secrets they're holding back, and `touched` the words that touched it. A secret is kept only if a word of `touched` is in the player's input or the last turn; otherwise it's dropped and counted, so secrets aren't in play every turn. A valid keep is saved on the turn (`kept`).
- **Secrets on the sheet** (`secrets.ts`, milestone 3.5; lore injection.md §7.1): `secretDisplay` shows every held secret as a short cue ("in short": a linked secret's holder part and codex summary, a personal secret's `summary` or first sentence) and some in full. Linked and personal secrets are ranked apart: kept last turn, then their own keywords in the turn's scan, then their subject brought up in the conversation (a linked secret's faction or `related` entries, or a codex entry named in a personal secret's text, among the entries the turn scan triggers; background lore of the place and the people present doesn't count). Ties go to the most recently kept, then the id. Each kind has its own per-character budget (the ruleset's `codex.linkedSecretTokens` and `codex.personalSecretTokens`), charged only the extra text of the full form; what doesn't fit stays a cue. A secret kept from its cue still reaches the writer in full. The display is in the lore trace (`secrets`) and Turn details.
- `moves` is the conversation in order: who, what it `answers`, a kind of move (answer, ask, push back, dodge, …), the ids of facts it may draw on (`uses`, a pool, not a script; a move may have none), anything `new` it brings in (a price, a time), and a check it `needs`.
- `settlePlan` runs after the checks are rolled: a move whose `needs` check failed is dropped (catching a lie takes the roll), and replies to it answer what it answered. A kept secret's tell goes to the writer the first time in a scene (`scene.tells`), or again when someone else's check that turn succeeded.
- `change` says what is different by the end of the turn. Texture is at most one detail.

`facts.ts` gives every knowledge line and secret an id (`<character>.k<n>`, `<character>.<secret>`), the place's news `place.<n>`, and what has been said on screen `said.<n>`. A move may use only its speaker's facts, the place's, or what has been said (`canSay`); anything else is dropped from the move and counted. `applyTurn` adds what was said to the scene's `told` and `newFacts`, so the next turn marks a fact new to the player only the first time, and anyone present can repeat it after.

**Repetition tracker** (`repeats.ts`): each character's slang (from their voice card) and gestures (a verb after their name in narration) from the last `RECENT_USE_TURNS` turns go to the writer as "Used in the last turns (don't repeat)"; a draft that repeats `REPEAT_REWRITE` or more of them gets the rewrite (`repeated`). `usageOf` counts slang, aim words and gestures in each turn's beats for the trace (`usage`) and the metrics.

The writer's prompt (`writer.ts`) is a few principles and one example. It gets what the player has been told, the moves with the facts they may draw on (each marked when new to the player), each speaker's goal and mood, their aim when it presses, a kept secret and its tell when it shows, the change, the check results and what was used lately. `exchange` is filled from the moves, so traces, read-outs and metrics read it the way they read logs from before milestone 3.6, when the line plan wrote it directly.
