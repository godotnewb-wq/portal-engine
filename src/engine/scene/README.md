# engine/scene

One player turn, in at most three LLM calls (`runTurn`):

1. **Director** (`director.ts`): reads everything about the scene and returns a JSON plan (`plan.ts`; its checked shape is in `planShape.ts`). The game checks it against the scene and the ruleset (`validatePlan`); an unreadable reply means the game plans the turn itself (`fallbackPlan`), at no extra call. The plan's `moves` are the turn's conversation in order (see **The plan**, below); `exchange` is derived from them, one line per move, for read-outs (`planLines`, `lineText`) and the beat trace, where a dialogue beat links to its `line:<n>` and to the check its move `needs`. Reactions are silent: quoted speech of three or more words in a narration beat is a `speech-in-narration` problem, and if the rewrite still has it, salvage cuts the words and keeps the action (`withoutSpeech`). A speaker's beats in a row, with only narration between, are one planned line written in parts. Anyone silent for `QUIET_TURNS` turns is listed in the director's turn prompt with their trade and aims, so the director can see whether the conversation touches them; whether they speak stays its call (no quotas).
2. **Game resolution** (`resolve.ts`): rolls the plan's checks with the seeded dice.
3. **Writer** (`writer.ts`): sees the plan as a brief, this turn's speakers' portraits and voice cards, its style principles and the writer's stage (the pack's tone and the names it may use); streams one beat per line with hidden tags (`beats.ts`). A beat is speech or narration, never both: a dialogue beat holds only the quoted words, and a character's actions are narration beats that name them. A line that still mixes the two is split by code into beats in order (`ParsedReply.reshaped`, `TurnTrace.writer.reshaped`), with no rewrite.
4. **Code checks** (`checks.ts`): speaking or acting for the player, unplanned or unknown speakers, over-used phrases (the engine's and the pack's), beat length and count. With a codex, `loreProblems` adds the name checks: a `wrong-name` (the fix names the right one) and an `invented-name` (use an existing name or a description); salvage lets names stand. The secret-leak check was removed on 2026-11-12: a secret can come out through anyone or anything the story allows, and conflicts with established lore are milestone 8's lore audit to sort out. The pack's names are indexed once (`packNames`). The director's lore lines get the same name check: a line with a wrong or invented name is dropped as a correction. Any hit means one rewrite; a rewrite that still fails is salvaged by code, never a fourth call.
5. **Apply** (`turn.ts`): the turn goes into the state with moods, comfort, expressions and departures. Moods come from the plan's `feels`; departures and the scene's end come from the plan alone. `acceptTags` decides which of the writer's tags count: a `[LEAVES]` tag moves no one, and when salvage cut every beat a character appeared in, their `EXPRESSION` and `COMFORT` tags are set aside. The `MOOD` tag was retired in milestone 4b; one written anyway is dropped as unparsed. Each tag set aside goes into the trace's `tags.dropped` with why. The writer's brief lists who leaves this turn; a planned departure the kept beats don't show (no line by them, no narration naming them) gets one narration beat in the pack's words (`showDepartures`, the ruleset's `narration.departure`), traced as a `game` beat carrying `leaves:<id>`.

**Director briefs** (`brief.ts`, milestone 4a; director writer §7, relationships §3): `buildBriefs` gives every character present an always-on brief (who they are, the portrait's first sentence as a cue, their `nature` and what they care about in words, tonight's concern, mood and comfort, feelings and ties toward the player and the others here, and for a Major Character the close-ties line of up to `briefs.closeTies` people not here), then the 3.5 secret display, the lying tell, memories and what the player already knows. Everything else on the card is optional detail chosen by `selectDetails` with no model call: knowledge (by fact id, so moves can cite it), long-term goals, contradictions, history notes for pairs present, ties to people not here, motives, and memories not recalled for the scene, faded ones included (milestone 4b; they come in on the player's words or last turn's, and the turn records them, `Turn.memories`, so the summary can name them as revisited). The trace reports each character's recalled-memory tokens apart (`memories`). Each candidate is ranked `answer` (it matches the player's words, names someone the player named, or a tie word the player said about them, "your sister"), `choice` (it matches last turn's lines, the pair both spoke last turn, a motive of someone addressed, or it was carried) or `texture` (a haunt of someone not here, the running summary), then by who was addressed and how strongly it matched. All present share the ruleset's `briefs.detailTokens`; ties to people not here are capped per character and per scene. Matching uses content words (four letters or more, stop words and names left out, roughly stemmed): one shared word with the player's input, two (one longer) with last turn's lines. `carryDetails` saves on the turn (`details`) what a move used, and what answered the player when the exchange ended on something open; the next turn keeps it on a follow-up (a short reply, or one that picks up last turn's words) and drops it on a change of topic. The trace's `brief` records each character's always-on tokens, the memories recalled (`recalled`, always set, so an empty one means none), every detail chosen with its rank, trigger and tokens, what the budget or a cap cut, and what was dropped. The writer gets this turn's speakers' ties toward the player and the others present (`speakerTies`): tags in the card's words, and the history note between two speakers or toward the player; never meter bands or private tags.

**Felt vs. shown** (`feelings.ts`, milestone 4b; npc design §7): the director owns felt emotion. Each speaker's `feels` (and a reaction's, when something lands on them) is a shade, its family when the shade has several, an intensity of 1–5 and a reason; `validatePlan` checks it against the ruleset (`readFeeling`) and refuses an unknown shade, a wrong or missing family, or an intensity off 1–5 with a correction, so the feeling from before carries. An intensity outside the shade's usual range is kept and marked `atypical` for tuning. The scene's `moods` are feelings: the card's default mood at the start, then the plan's `feels`. `shown` (given the turn's plan where there is one) works out visibility = intensity − masking + audience: masking is the card's word for the family, else its masking-axis stage's default (`maskingOf`); the audience (`audienceOf`) is the ruleset's `guarded` amount when anyone present is a stranger (a character with no saved relation and no tag, or a walk-on on screen: one with a line in an earlier turn or brought in by this turn's plan, `watchingWalkOns`; a walk-on who is only part of the location watches no one), someone they hold a `guardedTags` tag toward, or a member of a faction whose stance toward theirs is among the coldest `hostileStances`; `trusted` when everyone present, the player included, is above `trust` on `trustMeter`. The ruleset's `bands` turn the number into a sentence; the writer gets each speaker's and silent character's feeling in words, that sentence, and the card's display line (a shade's line over its family's), never a number; the director's brief shows the band. **Reading checks**: a check may name a `target`; the player rolls to read only the person `player_action.reading` names, the director's word for who the player is trying to read (watching, studying, looking for a lie; milestone 4d), and a player's read of anyone else is dropped with a correction, so noticing without trying is a companion's read; one with the ruleset's `people.reading.skill` gets its difficulty from the game (`readingDifficulty`: the target's masking for what they feel now, a step harder for the masking-axis stages `reading.harder` names), overriding the director's word with a correction. A reading check that succeeds adds a `read` (`settlePlan`): the writer is told that the reader sees through to the feeling, the turn keeps it (`Turn.reads`), and the reader's end-of-scene summary is told what they saw through to.

**Tie changes** (milestone 4b; relationships §3): not the director's. Relationship tags change only at the end of the scene: since milestone 4c each witness states them in their relationship call and the game settles what they said (`@engine/relationships`, `ties.ts`); the director prompt only says to play such events out. The rules a change must pass (`tieProblem`, `applyTie`, `answerTie`, `tieQuestion`) stay in `@engine/state`.

**Lore** (`lore.ts`, milestone 3): `turnLore` works out the turn's codex layers once (`@engine/codex`). The director opens with the **stage** (the pack's primer plus every common entry's stage line) in place of the bare primer, then gets the **scene-scoped layer** (the place's map chain, the factions and peoples of those present, linked entries as summaries) after the location, and the **triggered layer** (entries whose words came up in the input, the last turns or the summary) in its turn prompt. The writer gets the **writer's stage**: the tone and the names it may use. `turnLore` takes the player's lore budget overrides (`TurnOptions.loreBudgets`, from Settings or the bench config) over the pack's. The trace's `lore` lists the entry ids per layer, their rough tokens, and the budgets in effect with which the player set. The layers show each entry's id, so the director can pick **lore lines**: a turn-level pool of at most the turn's `loreLines` budget of plain facts (the ruleset's `codex.loreLines`, or the player's setting; with 0 the director isn't asked for any) (`{entry, who, line}`), apart from the moves. `validatePlan` checks them the way `canSay` checks facts: the entry was given this turn and isn't a secret; an insider entry's line needs a `who`, a speaker who knows it; failures are dropped and counted as corrections; a line over `LORE_LINE_WORDS` gives way to its entry's summary, also counted as a correction. Speakers may carry a `framing`, how they colour what they say about the world. The writer gets the lines under their own heading, to draw on if they fit (marked with who would say an insider one), and each speaker's framing on their line of the brief.

**The bookkeeper** (`bookkeeper.ts`, milestone 4c task 1; milestone-4 plan Part 4c, decisions J–L): one call after each turn is shown, while the player reads, that records what the turn did on screen. `runTurn` doesn't call it: the turn is shown first, and the app (`@platform/roles`' `bookkeepWithin`) and the bench run it after and apply its records with `applyRecords` before the next turn. `buildBookkeeperPrompt` gives it the people present, every secret held or learned by them or known to the player (`secretCandidates`: whatever was said, so a paraphrase or a bare "yes" can be recognized), each named by what is hidden (a linked secret's codex title, a personal one's summary or first sentence) and then in full, with the rule that only the hidden part counts and the background around it (a job, a place, a price, what people there know) is not the secret, the turn's checks, the turn before, and this turn's player words and numbered beats; not the director's `change` or `feels`, so its records are its own reading. It replies with secret observations (`at` the player's words or a line, `how`: `stated`, `guess`, `around`, `evidence` or `unclear`, the `words`, and for a contextual "yes" what it `answers`), new details, what `changed`, `moods` and whether each check was honored. `checkBookkeeping` decides authority: an observation needs a listed secret, a real line and its words found there (punctuation, case and ellipses aside), or in exactly one other line, where it is moved; who said it comes from the beat. An `answers` that isn't before it is left out, keeping the observation. In order, from what each knew going in (`knowersOf`): a secret said as known by its holder (not keeping it that turn), by someone who knew it, or by the player who knew it, or shown by the prose, comes out in front of everyone present (`out`, `confirmed` or `discovered`), and anyone may say it after; a guess, even a right one, teaches no one, and neither does talk `around` it (asking, dodging, refusing, denying, hinting; since 2026-11-19, so a denial has a kind of its own and isn't read as stating the secret), and neither does a statement by a player who didn't know it; said as known by anyone else, or by narration without showing it, it is a `leak`, the speaker's claim, teaching no one; a holder saying what they were keeping, or a case the bookkeeper couldn't tell, is `unresolved`. New details are limited by the ruleset's `bookkeeper.newDetails`, can't repeat what was said, and can't carry a claim or guess about a secret. Moods are checked against the ruleset's shades and checks against the turn's, for the logs only. `planBookkeeping` is the fallback (decision L): the only secret record the plan gives is a holder's voiced reveal or concede of their own secret, not kept, and the turn is `unaudited`. `bookkeep` runs the call (role `bookkeeper`) and falls back on a failed or unreadable reply; waiting for it is the caller's. The records' shape is `@engine/state`'s `Bookkeeping`.

Every turn also returns a **turn trace** (since milestone 4d, with `shown`: each feeling present with its masking, audience and why, visibility and band, as the writer was told; and `brief.recalled`: the memories recalled into each brief, by id) (`trace.ts`, milestone 2 A1): identity, the cast before and after, the director's full prompt and raw reply and how it was read, the plan after corrections, a breakdown of every check, the writer's and rewrite's prompts and replies with timings, the code findings and what salvage did, each beat with its source line and the plan item it carries out, the tags, and the cost. The engine builds it; the platform writes it (`@platform/logs`). A failed call throws a `TracedError` with the calls made so far.

**Assists and disclosure** (`resolve.ts`, milestone 4a; core rules §1, npc design §6): when a check resolves, `helpersFor` finds the party members present whose assist gate is met toward whoever rolls (the player, or another companion) and who have the ruleset's rank in the skill; `assistBonus` gives the bonus by how many help, never an edge, and the check line names them. `willingHelpers` puts the gate in words for the director ("Ada would back the player up"). A speaker who uses one of their own secrets in a move is checked by `wouldDisclose` against that secret's `disclose` minimums toward whoever the move is said to; a refusal drops the secret with a correction. The director's sheet says in words whether a secret with minimums would be told to the player now. A secret already said on screen, or already known to the player, isn't gated.

**The child guard** (`guard.ts`, director writer §5): when a character whose age stage the ruleset lists under `people.ages.minors` is present, `childGuard` adds an engine line to the director's and the writer's system prompts naming them and keeping them out of anything romantic or sexual; packs can't turn it off. The director's sheet header names a minor's age stage.

## The plan
The situation plan (`director.ts`, `plan.ts`, `facts.ts`, `writer.ts`; `director writer review.md`, option B; the only pipeline since milestone 3.6 removed the older line plan, which planned each line's point): the director plans what is true, wanted and at stake, and writes no prose or dialogue.
- `player_action` says what the player is doing and whether it is an uncertain attempt; a player check without one is dropped.
- Each speaker has a `goal` (what they want in this exchange) and a `pressure`: one of the ruleset's pressure words for how hard their aim for the scene presses. It moves at most one step a turn (`scene.pressures`). The writer sees the aim only above the weakest word.
- `keeps` lists the ids of the speaker's own secrets they're holding back, and `touched` the words that touched it. A secret is kept only if a word of `touched` is in the player's input or the last turn; otherwise it's dropped and counted, so secrets aren't in play every turn. A valid keep is saved on the turn (`kept`).
- **Learned and dormant secrets** (milestone 4b; since milestone 4c, the turn's records): `Turn.exposed` holds the secrets that came out on screen this turn, in front of everyone present but their holder, as the turn's records give them (`applyRecords`): the bookkeeper's, or until it answers and when it fails the plan's, which give only a holder's voiced reveal or concede of their own secret, not kept. A reading check no longer gives the reader the secret; it records that the one read was hiding something (`Read.hiding`), which their memory call is told. The player's keyword rule and the 4b answer and telling rules are gone (decision L). The end-of-scene summary turns only these into learned secrets. A secret learned in play is a fact its learner can say too (`Fact.knownBy`, `canSay`), holder present or not, and a candidate for their brief's optional detail by its fact id; `knowerIn` adds the codex pointers on their memories to the knowledge filter. On the holder's sheet a secret is kept only from those present who don't know it ("Already known to …"); once everyone present knows it, it is off (`SecretShown.off: 'known'`). Of the rest, the ruleset's `codex.activeSecrets` most recently active are on (pinned first, then those brought up this turn, then by `state.secretActivity`, then the card's order); the others are dormant (`off: 'dormant'`) until a keyword or subject brings them back. `activeSecrets` and the turn's keeps update `state.secretActivity`.
- **Secrets on the sheet** (`secrets.ts`, milestone 3.5; lore injection.md §7.1): `secretDisplay` shows every held secret as a short cue ("in short": a linked secret's holder part and codex summary, a personal secret's `summary` or first sentence) and some in full. Linked and personal secrets are ranked apart: kept last turn, then their own keywords in the turn's scan, then their subject brought up in the conversation (a linked secret's faction or `related` entries, or a codex entry named in a personal secret's text, among the entries the turn scan triggers; background lore of the place and the people present doesn't count). Ties go to the most recently kept, then the id. Each kind has its own per-character budget (the ruleset's `codex.linkedSecretTokens` and `codex.personalSecretTokens`), charged only the extra text of the full form; what doesn't fit stays a cue. A secret kept from its cue still reaches the writer in full. The display is in the lore trace (`secrets`) and Turn details.
- `moves` is the conversation in order: who, what it `answers`, a kind of move (answer, ask, push back, dodge, …), the ids of facts it may draw on (`uses`, a pool, not a script; a move may have none), anything `new` it brings in (a price, a time), and a check it `needs`.
- `settlePlan` runs after the checks are rolled: a move whose `needs` check failed is dropped (catching a lie takes the roll), and replies to it answer what it answered. A kept secret's tell goes to the writer the first time in a scene (`scene.tells`), or again when someone else's check that turn succeeded.
- `change` says what is different by the end of the turn. Texture is at most one detail.

`facts.ts` gives every knowledge line and secret an id (`<character>.k<n>`, `<character>.<secret>`), the place's news `place.<n>`, and what has been said on screen `said.<n>`. A move may use only its speaker's facts, the place's, or what has been said (`canSay`); anything else is dropped from the move and counted. `applyTurn` adds the known facts a voiced move drew on to the scene's `told` (plan-based until milestone 5), and `applyRecords` adds the turn's secrets that came out and its new details (`newFacts`, each with the turn that said it), so the next turn marks a fact new to the player only the first time, and anyone present can repeat it after. `applyRecords` replaces a turn's records whole, so the bookkeeper's take the place of the plan's, and applying them twice changes nothing.

**The `scene` pipeline's gathering** (`gather.ts`, Milestone 5 task 1; scene system redesign §4–5): before the director plans, the game works out what could be live, so the director chooses among real options rather than marking a secret every turn. For each character present: tonight's aim at its strength (the card's `agenda.pressure`, else the weakest word, until the director moves it a step a turn); the secrets something just said touches (in the player's words or the last turn: a keyword, the subject, or someone the secret names who isn't here; a secret everyone present knows is off); ties in play (toward the player, toward someone here, or toward someone just named). What was live in the ruleset's `scene.recentTurns` last turns comes from `Turn.live`. Each character's **fact pool**, at most `scene.factPool`, best first: what the brief brought in for them by rule (knowledge and memories the conversation reached), lore just named that they know, memories recalled for the scene, the place's news. A fact the player was told comes back only when asked about again; secrets are never in it.

**The scene director** (`sceneDirector.ts`, Milestone 5 task 2; redesign §4): a game master, not a playwright. Short rules and one made-up example; the same world, place, people and scene so far as the situation director (`directorContext`, `sceneSoFar`); then what the game gathered: for each person, tonight's aim at its strength, the secrets something just touched, who could see through them, the ties in play and what was live lately; each person's fact pool by id, with the place's news listed once; and what the bookkeeper recorded as changed last turn. It returns the scene plan below.

**Who could see through someone** (`readersOf`, task 2): for a holder of a secret something just touched, anyone present who knows them (a tie or a history note toward them), best reading bonus first, at most two. No run before Milestone 5 had a companion read: the director was told to give the read to whoever "could see through it" and never told who. A live secret with a reader present and no read on its holder gets a correction.

**The scene plan** (`scenePlan.ts`): focus (at most three), a moment for each (`feels` plus how it lands and what they want now, in words), aim strengths, at most one live pressure each from the gathered options, the facts each draws on from their pool, the stakes (in place of `change`), checks, walk-ons, departures and the avoid list. No moves, no order, no wording. `validateScenePlan` checks it against the gathering; every change is a correction.

**Repetition tracker** (`repeats.ts`): each character's slang (from their voice card) and gestures (a verb after their name in narration) from the last `RECENT_USE_TURNS` turns go to the writer as "Used in the last turns (don't repeat)"; a draft that repeats `REPEAT_REWRITE` or more of them gets the rewrite (`repeated`). `usageOf` counts slang, aim words and gestures in each turn's beats for the trace (`usage`) and the metrics.

**Who knows what** (milestone 4c task 3; decision L, 2026-11-18, user; no secret screen: nothing in the director's notes is cut). The writer's style says what each person knows comes first: someone who doesn't know a secret may ask, guess, suspect or deny it, never say it as known, and narration never confirms it unless the turn shows it. `knowledgeLines` gives the writer a "Who knows what" section: for each secret the brief gives it (kept by a speaker, or one a move may draw on), who here knows it and who doesn't; and for a secret it doesn't give, a reminder without its content when a director note (a speaker's aim, a move's new detail, the turn's change) uses one of its keywords. No line once everyone here knows it or it was said on screen. The one rewrite reuses the brief, so it gets the same lines. **Claims:** `claimLines` lists each leak the bookkeeper recorded this scene (someone said, as if they knew it, a secret they couldn't know) that nobody has confirmed since; the director and the writer get them under "Claimed but never confirmed", so others can doubt or deny them, and the scene-end calls get them too. The director is told a plan gives nobody knowledge: asking, guessing, suspecting or denying another's secret needs nothing in `uses`. **Trim** (run 2's corrections): a reading check needs no difficulty (the game sets it and corrects only a word that differs), the pressure step limit and the reading check's difficulty sentence leave the rules (the game enforces both), the two "people are in the moment" rules and the two move-answering rules are merged, and the director's shade list gives words only, without their usual intensities.

The writer's prompt (`writer.ts`) is a few principles and one example. It gets what the player has been told, the moves with the facts they may draw on (each marked when new to the player), each speaker's goal, what they feel and how much of it shows, their aim when it presses, a kept secret and its tell when it shows, the change, the check results and what was used lately. `exchange` is filled from the moves, so traces, read-outs and metrics read it the way they read logs from before milestone 3.6, when the line plan wrote it directly.
